What this app can do

Most of it is a tap you would not guess was there.

Departures

Typea station to see what is leaving. The board refreshes itself every 30 seconds.
☆stars a station so it sits one tap away next time.
Tap a rowto see where that train actually goes — every stop, with times.
Tap the destinationto plan a journey there on that train, not a generic search from now.

Journey

A → Bthe chips above the two fields are routes you have searched before, newest first. Nothing to save — a search records itself. The two directions are kept apart, because the morning trip and the evening one are not the same trip.
⇅swaps the two stations.
Now· Leave at · Arrive by — Arrive by works backwards from when you must be there.
🌇 Sunseta fourth way to say when: arrive before the sun goes down at the place you are going, on the day you are going. It looks the sunset up there, not here, and fills in the arrive-by time for you. Tap it after dark and it plans for tomorrow instead. The forecast only reaches two days out, so for anything further it says so rather than quietly answering about today.
🌅 Sunrisethe same thing at the other end of the day, and it usually means tomorrow: it plans to have you there before first light, which is the point of going up a mountain early. Tapping it after the sun is already up rolls to the next day by itself. If nothing gets you there that early it says so plainly — that is the first train of the day, not a mistake in your search.
Pl. 7is the platform you leave from. Amber with ⚠ means it changed from the scheduled one.
7→5on a change means you arrive at 7 and leave from 5 — you have to cross the station.
Tap a leg(the coloured badge) for the stops in between.
🗺 mapsketches the whole route, with a pulsing dot for where the train is now.
⛰under the sketch: the same journey seen from the side, so a valley run and a mountain climb are one glance apart. It is the ground height at each stop joined by straight lines — terrain, not railway data, and nothing about the track’s own gradient. Routes with too few stops to say anything get no strip at all.
🚞 to the topwhen a leg is a cable car, gondola, cog railway or funicular you are going up, and the only question is whether it is worth it today — cloud at the summit ruins the whole point, and a temperature will not tell you that. Shows the height, the forecast for the hour you arrive, and a plain verdict. City funiculars are left out: a ride to a university terrace is not an excursion.
⛰ on the wayunder the same sketch: places worth breaking the journey for, each pinned to a stop the train actually calls at and the time it gets there. Only things within a few kilometres of a stop are listed — a peak you would need a four-hour hike to reach is not a stop-off. The first and last stations are left out, because you are going there anyway.
🚢 Boatthe mode chips under the time row. Tap one and only journeys using it are shown — a Luzern–Rütli search turns from a bus-and-train slog into the direct lake steamer. They stay set until you tap ✕ all modes, so if a search comes back empty it will tell you the filter is why.

The two switches

Smartsweeps the hubs for connections the default search misses, and flags changes too tight to trust.
Scenicfloats panoramic routes to the top when one exists.
Weathershows the forecast at your departure hour and at your arrival hour — the two that matter.
