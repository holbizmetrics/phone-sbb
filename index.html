What this app can do

Most of it is a tap you would not guess was there.

Departures

Typea station to see what is leaving. The board refreshes itself every 30 seconds.
☆stars a station so it sits one tap away next time.
Tap a rowto see where that train actually goes — every stop, with times.
Tap the destinationto plan a journey there on that train, not a generic search from now.
🌙next to the station name: what still moves from here tonight — each line’s last run, in the order they expire. If nothing moves any more it says exactly that, which at 01:00 is the answer you need.

Journey

A → Bthe chips above the two fields are routes you have searched before, newest first. Nothing to save — a search records itself. The two directions are kept apart, because the morning trip and the evening one are not the same trip.
⇅swaps the two stations.
↷route via a stop you name. The change-finder stands down while it is set — you chose the change, so it stops offering others. An empty result then means “not in that order”, not “no route”.
📍in an empty From field fills in the nearest stop to where you are standing — it asks for your location only when you tap it, never on its own.
↗ Shareabove the results sends the search to a friend — WhatsApp, wherever. Their phone looks the timetable up live when they open it, so they see current trains, not a screenshot of yours. A shared time that has already passed is quietly replaced with “now”.
▲ ▼at the left of that same row walk the timetable earlier and later. Each step is a fresh look-up that keeps your via and your train-type filter, and it moves the “Leave at” time so you can always see which window you are looking at. Step past the first or last train of the day and it says so, instead of handing you the same list again.
Now· Leave at · Arrive by — Arrive by works backwards from when you must be there.
🌇 Sunseta fourth way to say when: arrive before the sun goes down at the place you are going, on the day you are going. It looks the sunset up there, not here, and fills in the arrive-by time for you. Tap it after dark and it plans for tomorrow instead. The forecast only reaches two days out, so for anything further it says so rather than quietly answering about today.
🌅 Sunrisethe same thing at the other end of the day, and it usually means tomorrow: it plans to have you there before first light, which is the point of going up a mountain early. Tapping it after the sun is already up rolls to the next day by itself. If nothing gets you there that early it says so plainly — that is the first train of the day, not a mistake in your search.
✈ Flightworks backwards from a flight: you give the departure time, you say how early you want to be at the airport, and it plans the trains to land you there. It will not pick that number for you — nothing is planned until you tap one. This app knows trains; it does not know your airline’s check-in deadline, your bag drop, the queue at security, or the walk from the platform to the desk, and it says so every time. The time on your booking wins. If your destination does not look like an airport station it warns you rather than assuming.
🤝 Meet in the middletreats the two fields as two people’s starting points and names the stations where your ride times come out closest, fairest first — with both clock times and when you would both be there. Tap my leg or their leg to plan either half; the share bar sends theirs on. It reads the two direct connections rather than hammering the timetable, so it costs two requests, and it only ever runs when you tap it.
Pl. 7is the platform you leave from. Amber with ⚠ means it changed from the scheduled one.
7→5on a change means you arrive at 7 and leave from 5 — you have to cross the station.
☕ step out?appears on a change of twenty minutes or more: what is within a short walk of that station — a café, a bakery, a viewpoint — with one-way walking times scoped to the minutes you actually have, keeping ten in reserve to find your platform again. Nothing nearby is said plainly, and a map outage is an outage, not a “no”.
📌 I'm on this onepins the journey you actually boarded to a bar that stays with you across tabs and reloads — no GPS, no permission prompt. Tap the bar for the next change: arrival and departure platforms, and whether the minutes are enough to leave the platform or you should stay on it. Nearby spots during long changes are optional — a toggle inside the sheet, off until you switch it on.
Tap a leg(the coloured badge) for the stops in between.
🗺 mapsketches the whole route, with a pulsing dot for where the train is now.
⛰under the sketch: the same journey seen from the side, so a valley run and a mountain climb are one glance apart. It is the ground height at each stop joined by straight lines — terrain, not railway data, and nothing about the track’s own gradient. Routes with too few stops to say anything get no strip at all.
🚞 to the topwhen a leg is a cable car, gondola, cog railway or funicular you are going up, and the only question is whether it is worth it today — cloud at the summit ruins the whole point, and a temperature will not tell you that. Shows the height, the forecast for the hour you arrive, and a plain verdict — then the same verdict for each of the next seven days, with the best one named. Tap a day to plan the trip for it. Beyond about three days cloud is a tendency, not a promise. City funiculars are left out: a ride to a university terrace is not an excursion.
⛰ on the wayunder the same sketch: places worth breaking the journey for, each pinned to a stop the train actually calls at and the time it gets there. Only things within a few kilometres of a stop are listed — a peak you would need a four-hour hike to reach is not a stop-off. The first and last stations are left out, because you are going there anyway.
🚢 Boatthe mode chips under the time row. Tap one and only journeys using it are shown — a Luzern–Rütli search turns from a bus-and-train slog into the direct lake steamer. They stay set until you tap ✕ all modes, so if a search comes back empty it will tell you the filter is why.

The two switches

Smartsweeps the hubs for connections the default search misses, and flags changes too tight to trust.
Scenicfloats panoramic routes to the top when one exists.
Weathershows the forecast at your departure hour and at your arrival hour — the two that matter.

Elsewhere

◑switches light and dark. Dark is the default and it stays where you put it.
●this dot — opens this page.
Live timetable data comes from transport.opendata.ch, so the app needs a connection to fetch, though what is already on screen keeps working. The moving dot on the sketch is the scheduled position worked out from the timetable, not GPS — it shows where the train should be, which is not always where it is.

Privacy

No account, no ads, no analytics, no cookies. Your favourites, routes and the pinned journey live in this browser’s own storage and never leave the phone. The app talks to exactly four services, each with only what the question needs: station names and times to transport.opendata.ch, coordinates to Open-Meteo (weather), Overpass/OSM (nearby places), and Wikipedia (sights); links to maps, webcams or a second forecast open in a new tab — what you do there is between you and them. Your location is read only in the moment you tap 📍, and is sent nowhere except that one nearest-stop lookup. There is nothing to log in to because there is nothing about you here.
